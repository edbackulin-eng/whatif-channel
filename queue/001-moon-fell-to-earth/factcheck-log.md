## Changes applied (8)

### 1. Roche limit presented as a single figure, and on the wrong side of the satellites

- **Was:** around eighteen thousand kilometres from Earth's centre ... It is above most of our satellites' territory
- **Now:** somewhere between about nine and a half thousand and eighteen thousand kilometres from Earth's centre ... even the near end is thousands of kilometres up
- **Why:** Two errors. The claim that 18,000 km is above most satellites is simply wrong - geostationary is 42,164 km from Earth's centre, so the Moon would be well BELOW it, and the script's own earlier paragraph already said the Moon wrecks those orbits on the way down. Separately, 18,400 km is the FLUID Roche limit; the rigid-body value for a solid Moon is ~9,480 km. The honest statement is the range.
- **Source:** d = 1.26R(rho_E/rho_m)^(1/3) rigid and 2.44R fluid, with R=6371 km and densities 5514/3344 kg/m3; ESA orbit definitions for geostationary altitude

### 2. The Moon's role in stabilising Earth's axial tilt was presented as settled

- **Was:** Take our Moon away and Earth's tilt is free to drift ... The tropics move. The poles move. Ice sheets form in places that were temperate ... on a schedule nothing alive can adapt to.
- **Now:** an explicit statement that this is contested, giving both the 1993 chaotic-swing result and the later simulations finding a moonless Earth confined to roughly a 20-25 degree range
- **Why:** This is the correction I am least comfortable having needed. Laskar, Joutel & Robutel (1993) found a moonless Earth could wander chaotically up to ~85 degrees, which is the version that entered popular science. Lissauer, Barnes & Chambers (2012) re-ran it and found variation typically confined to about 20-25 degrees, concluding a large moon is not needed to stabilise an Earth-like planet. Waltham (2013) disputes both. Stating the dramatic version as fact would have been the single least defensible claim in the video.
- **Source:** Laskar, Joutel & Robutel, Nature 361 (1993); Lissauer, Barnes & Chambers, Icarus 217 (2012); Waltham, EPSC 2013

### 3. Solid Earth tide amplitude understated

- **Was:** rises and falls by about thirty centimetres twice a day, then roughly twenty to thirty centimetres
- **Now:** roughly thirty to forty centimetres twice a day
- **Why:** The main lunar M2 vertical component is 384.83 mm, with the full body tide reaching into the 50 cm range at some latitudes. My first figure was a round guess and my first correction moved it the wrong way.
- **Source:** Earth tide constituent table, M2 vertical amplitude 384.83 mm

### 4. Light travel time stated too precisely for a range of distances

- **Was:** about a tenth of a second, then about six hundredths of a second
- **Now:** a few hundredths of a second
- **Why:** The breakup happens across a range of distances, which spans roughly 0.038 to 0.060 s. A single decimal figure implies precision the scenario does not have.
- **Source:** c = 299,792 km/s

### 5. Tidal interval rounded to twelve hours

- **Was:** Then it does it again twelve hours later.
- **Now:** Then it does it again about twelve and a half hours later.
- **Why:** The semidiurnal tidal period is 12h25m, which is why tides drift later each day - a detail that matters in a video about tides.
- **Source:** NOAA National Ocean Service, tidal cycle

### 6. Saturn ring span

- **Was:** over two hundred and seventy thousand kilometres across
- **Now:** over two hundred and eighty thousand kilometres across
- **Why:** The main rings span roughly 282,000 km in diameter.
- **Source:** NASA Cassini ring science

### 7. Rate of day lengthening

- **Was:** roughly one and a half to two milliseconds per century
- **Now:** roughly two milliseconds per century
- **Why:** Long-baseline eclipse records give 1.7-2.3 ms/century; my lower bound was below the accepted range.
- **Source:** Stephenson & Morrison eclipse analysis; IERS

### 8. Lunar volcanic quiescence

- **Was:** a world that has been geologically quiet for three billion years
- **Now:** a world whose great volcanic era ended two to three billion years ago
- **Why:** Mare volcanism peaked 3.0-3.8 Ga and tapered by ~2.0-2.5 Ga, and the Moon is not wholly quiet today - LRO found young thrust faults and Apollo recorded shallow moonquakes.
- **Source:** Apollo sample dating; NASA Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (Watters et al. 2012)
